READER SUMMARY
The paper takes three questions from a lecture whiteboard about a finite, simple, undirected graph with N vertices and its 0/1 adjacency matrix A. (1) Entry (i, j) of A^k is the number of walks of length exactly k from vertex i to vertex j. It counts walks, not paths, because a walk may revisit vertices and edges; the three-vertex example shows this. The proof is by induction on k. (2) A graph with N ≥ 2 vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + ... + A^(N−1) is positive. This is equivalent to every entry of (I + A)^(N−1) being positive. The reason is that any walk between two vertices contains a path of length at most N−1. The cutoff N−1 cannot be lowered, as shown by the path graph. The paper compares the test with breadth-first search, which it says is cheaper, and checks both by computer on all small graphs. (3) A path between two vertices is a walk with no repeated vertex. The textbook calls this a "simple path" and uses "path" for a walk with no repeated edge (a trail). Both readings give the same notion of connected. I can state all this, but I took on faith the content of the whiteboard and assignment text, the computer checks, and the cost estimates. The role of Section 5 and the heavy hedging around Q3 were hard to follow.
