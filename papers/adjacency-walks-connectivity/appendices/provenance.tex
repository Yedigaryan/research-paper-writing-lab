\section{Sources, pages checked and how to rerun the computations}\label{app:provenance}

\subsection*{Sources and how much of each was read}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item The course textbook \cite{petrosyan2015graph}: read in part. We viewed
  printed pages 7, 11 and 16, pages 21 to 23, and pages 48 and 49 as images,
  and page 9 through its text layer, and we searched the text for the
  adjacency matrix. Printed page numbers are the PDF page minus one. The text
  layer drops most mathematical symbols, so formulas were taken from the page
  images.
  \item The two whiteboard photos \cite{whiteboard1,whiteboard2}: read
  together as one board.
  \item The Galway notes \cite{galwaynotes}, Gotti's notes
  \cite{gotti18211lecture27} and the journal article of Paramadevan and
  Sotheeswaran \cite{paramadevan2021adjacency}: read in full. Bai's notes
  \cite{bai2025adjacency}, Grinberg's book \cite{grinberg2025graph} and the
  Waterloo slides \cite{waterloocs341lecture10}: read in part, at the places
  cited. The Wikipedia page \cite{wikipedia2026path}: read in part, through a
  summarising tool, so its wording is as returned and not checked against the raw
  page.
\end{itemize}

All web sources were accessed on 2 November 2026 (see the bibliography).

\subsection*{The computations}

Every matrix in the text, every walk count and every classification of an
example sequence comes from one script, \texttt{appendices/scripts/paper\_computations.py}
\cite{paperscript}, run from the paper folder with
\begin{verbatim}
python3 -I appendices/scripts/paper_computations.py \
    > appendices/scripts/paper_computations_output.txt
\end{verbatim}
It writes \texttt{appendices/generated-matrices.tex}, which this document
inputs, so no matrix is typed by hand. It computes powers by the definition of
the matrix product and checks every entry of $A^k$ for $k=1,\dots,4$ against an
explicit enumeration of walks. The script
\texttt{appendices/scripts/m3\_examples.py} \cite{m3examples} computes the two
three-vertex examples independently, and
\texttt{appendices/scripts/m3\_exhaustive\_check.py} \cite{m3exhaustive}
performs the finite check of the test on all graphs with up to six vertices.
The script \texttt{appendices/scripts/m2\_paths.py} cuts every walk of length at
most $6$ in all $1099$ labelled simple graphs on at most five vertices and found
no violation of \cref{lem:walkpath}; its output is saved beside it. Run each
with \texttt{python3 -I}.
