\section{Sources, checks and how to rerun the computations}\label{app:provenance}

\paragraph{Sources and what was read.}
The \emph{course textbook} \cite{petrosyan2015grafneri} is a local file supplied
with the assignment. Its title is in Armenian script; the reference list gives a
transliteration because the document is typeset with a Latin font. We viewed the
page images of printed pages 11, 21, 22, 23, 48 and 49 before quoting
definitions, and read the extracted text of pages 16 and 22--23. The rest of the
book was searched, not read: a search of the extracted text for the word
``matrix'' finds the adjacency matrix only on printed pages 7, 11, 12, 34 and
61, and finds no theorem about the powers of $A$ and no matrix test for
connectivity. The extraction drops most symbols, so this does not prove that
no such statement exists. The two \emph{board photographs}
\cite{whiteboard1lecture,whiteboard2lecture} show the same board from two
angles and were read together. \emph{Grinberg's lecture notes}
\cite{grinberg2025introduction} were read in Section 2.9, in Theorem 4.5.10 and
its proof, and in the passage on breadth-first search. \emph{Paramadevan and
Sotheeswaran} \cite{paramadevan2021properties} were read in Section 2.2 (item 7) and
Section 3.1 (Theorems 1 to 3 and their proofs). The \emph{encyclopedia page}
\cite{wikipediapathgraphtheory} was read as raw wikitext on 2026-11-05 and is
used only for names. The \emph{computer check} \cite{m3connectivityscript} is
a short program written for this note; it computes the examples of
\cref{sec:connectivity} and the exhaustive comparison. The program
\texttt{appendices/computations.py} described below repeats those computations
and adds the others, so every number in the text can be rerun from that one
program. The text cites the first program for the examples, the
breadth-first search and the exhaustive check of \cref{sec:connectivity}, where
those results were first produced, and the second program reproduces them.

\paragraph{What is ours.} The statements and proofs of \cref{lem:shorten},
\cref{cor:readings}, \cref{prop:distance}, \cref{prop:properties}(b)--(d),
\cref{thm:count}, \cref{thm:test}, \cref{cor:IA}, \cref{cor:components},
and \cref{prop:sharp} are proved in this note. Where a source states the same
result, the text says which one. The reading of the board is recorded as
conventions 1 to 3 in \cref{sec:conventions}.

\paragraph{Rerunning the numbers.} Every matrix, count and example in the text
is recomputed, with exact integers, by the program
\texttt{appendices/computations.py} in the paper folder. From that folder,
run \texttt{python3 -I appendices/computations.py}; it takes about twenty
seconds, and its output is saved as \texttt{appendices/computations.out.txt}.
The program lists the matrices of \cref{ex:board}, \cref{ex:connected},
\cref{ex:disconnected} and \cref{ex:triangle}, the walks that these examples
name, the statuses (walk, trail, path, closed) of the examples in
\cref{sec:background}, the counts of \cref{tab:counts}, a check of the
binomial identity, a consistency check of the cutting rule of
\cref{lem:shorten} on all short walks of the five-town map, and the exhaustive
comparison with breadth-first search for $N\le6$. These checks support the
proofs and do not replace them.
