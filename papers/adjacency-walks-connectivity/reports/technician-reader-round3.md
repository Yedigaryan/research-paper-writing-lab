READER SUMMARY
The note asks three questions raised by a lecture board on graphs. First, what does the (i, j) entry of the k-th power of the adjacency matrix mean? It is the number of walks of exactly k edges from vertex i to vertex j. Walks may revisit vertices and reuse edges, so the entry does not count paths. It is proved by induction on k. Second, how can one tell from the matrix that a graph is connected? Form S = A + A² + ... + A^(N−1). The graph is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of S is positive, and equivalently when (I+A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. The proof uses the fact that any walk can be shortened to a path (by cutting out loops) and that a path has at most N−1 edges. The power N−1 cannot be reduced, as shown by a chain of N vertices. Third, what is a path? It is a walk with no repeated vertex, whereas the textbook's "path" means a walk with no repeated edge, which this note calls a trail. The three notions give the same connected graphs. Small examples and a computer check on all labelled graphs with up to 6 vertices support the results. I could follow this, but only after reading past a lot of source-comparison material.
