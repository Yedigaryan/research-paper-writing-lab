READER SUMMARY
The paper starts from a lecture-board photo that asks two things about a graph's adjacency matrix A (a table of 0s and 1s marking which points are joined by a line), plus a third question about what "path" means. It answers three questions. (Q1) For k ≥ 1, the entry in row i, column j of A multiplied by itself k times is the number of walks of exactly k steps from vertex i to vertex j. A walk may revisit points and lines, so the entry does not count paths, and the paper shows this with small counterexamples. (Q2) A graph with N ≥ 2 vertices is connected if and only if every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + … + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, it is connected if and only if the matrix (I + A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. The reason is that any walk can be shortened to a path with no repeated vertex, and such a path has at most N−1 steps. The paper proves this by induction and checks it by computer on all labelled graphs with up to six vertices. (Q3) By the course convention, a path between two vertices is a sequence of pairwise different vertices in which consecutive ones are adjacent. The textbook's own word "path" means a walk with no repeated line, which the paper calls a trail, and this difference does not change which graphs count as connected. The paper also notes that the board's drawing and matrix disagree by one edge. It follows the matrix and works a connected three-vertex example and a disconnected one.
