READER SUMMARY
The paper is a study note on adjacency matrices of simple undirected graphs, the zero-one tables recording which pairs of vertices are joined by an edge. It sets out to answer three questions taken from a lecture whiteboard and an assignment. (Q1) The entry in row i and column j of the k-th power of the matrix equals the number of walks of exactly k steps from vertex i to vertex j. A walk may repeat vertices and edges, so this counts walks, not paths. The diagonal of A squared equals the vertex degrees, which shows this. (Q2) A graph with N vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + … + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, (I + A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. The proof uses the fact that any walk contains a path, and that a path has at most N − 1 steps. (Q3) In the course, a path between two vertices is a sequence of adjacent vertices with no vertex repeated. The textbook's own word "path" means no repeated edge, which the note calls a trail. For connectivity, walks, trails and paths give the same reachable pairs. The note works two three-vertex examples, one connected and one disconnected. It reports that the board's drawing and matrix disagree by one edge, but both give a connected graph. It checks the results by computer on all labelled graphs with up to 6 vertices. It also argues that three sentences in other sources, which read these entries as path counts, are wrong when "path" has its standard meaning. I could state the summary, but only after skipping much of the convention, source and terminology bookkeeping.
