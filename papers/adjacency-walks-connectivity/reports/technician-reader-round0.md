READER SUMMARY
The note asks three questions that come from a lecture on graphs. (1) What does the entry in row i and column j of a power A^k of a graph's adjacency matrix mean? (2) How can you tell from A whether the graph is connected? (3) What is a "path" between two vertices? Its answers are as follows. (1) The entry counts the walks of exactly k edges from vertex i to vertex j. A walk may repeat vertices and edges, so the entry does not count paths. This is proved by induction on k. (2) A graph with at least 2 vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + … + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, (I + A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. The proof uses the fact that any walk contains a path that is no longer, and that a path has at most N−1 edges. Worked examples on a three-vertex connected graph and a three-vertex disconnected graph illustrate it, and a computer check up to 6 vertices agrees. (3) A path is a walk with no repeated vertex. The course textbook uses "path" for a walk with no repeated edge and "simple path" for no repeated vertex, and the note shows that the choice does not change which graphs count as connected. I could write this summary, but I had to take on trust everything that depends on the board photos, the textbook, the user's statements and the local scripts, because none of those are visible in the paper.
