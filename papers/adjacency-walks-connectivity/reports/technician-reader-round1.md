READER SUMMARY
The paper starts from a lecture board showing a three-town graph and its 0/1 adjacency matrix A. It answers three questions. First, the entry (Ak)ij of the k-th power of A is the number of walks of exactly k edges from vertex i to vertex j. Walks may revisit vertices and reuse edges, so the entry does not count paths. Second, a graph with N ≥ 2 vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + … + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, it is connected when (I+A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. The proof turns a walk into a path by cutting out loops, and uses the fact that a path has at most N−1 edges. Third, a "path" between two vertices is a walk with no repeated vertex. The note says the textbook's own word "path" means a walk with no repeated edge, which it calls a trail. The three readings give the same connected graphs. The note supports these results with proofs, two worked three-vertex examples, and a computer check on all graphs with up to 6 vertices. The reader could not verify the computer results or the textbook and board references.
