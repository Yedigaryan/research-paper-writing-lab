READER SUMMARY
The paper asks three questions about a graph's adjacency matrix A, which is a table of 0s and 1s. These questions appear to come from a lecture whiteboard and an assignment. The first question is what the entry (A^k)_ij means. It is the number of walks of exactly k steps from vertex i to vertex j. A walk may repeat vertices and edges, so this counts walks and not paths. The diagonal of A² equals the vertex degrees, which shows that walks and paths are different things. The second question is how to tell from A alone whether the graph is connected. The answer is that a graph with N vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + ... + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, every entry of (I+A)^(N−1) is positive. The proof shows that any walk can be shortened to a path with at most N−1 steps. The third question is what "path" means in the course. It means a walk with no repeated vertex. The textbook's own word "path" means a walk with no repeated edge, which the note calls a trail. For testing connectivity, walks, trails and paths give the same reachable pairs. The note works one connected and one disconnected three-vertex example. It notes that the board's drawing (three lines) disagrees with its matrix (two edges). It follows the matrix, and both readings are connected. It also checks small graphs by computer and points out three sentences in the literature that wrongly read matrix entries as path counts.
