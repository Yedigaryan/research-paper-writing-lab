READER SUMMARY
The paper starts from a lecture whiteboard that shows a small graph and its adjacency matrix. The graph is a set of points joined by lines, and the matrix is a table of 0s and 1s with a 1 where two points are joined. The board asks three questions. The first is what an entry of the k-th power of the matrix means. The paper answers that the entry (A^k)_ij is the number of walks of exactly k steps from vertex i to vertex j. It proves this by induction, splitting each walk by its last-but-one vertex. Walks may revisit vertices and repeat edges, so the entry does not count paths. Small examples with three vertices show this. The second question is how to tell from the matrix whether the graph is connected. The answer is a test. The graph on N ≥ 2 vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + … + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, the matrix (I + A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. The proof relies on the fact that any walk can be shortened to a path with no repeated vertex, and that a path has at most N−1 steps. The test was also checked by computer on all graphs with up to six vertices. The third question is the meaning of "path". Under the course convention, a path is a sequence of distinct vertices with consecutive ones adjacent. The textbook uses "path" for a trail, a walk with no repeated edge. The paper shows that the two meanings give the same connected graphs. It also notes that the board's drawing and matrix disagree on one edge, and it follows the matrix.
