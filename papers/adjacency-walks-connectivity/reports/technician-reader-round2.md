READER SUMMARY
The note answers three questions that came from a lecture. (1) What does the (i, j) entry of the k-th power of a graph's adjacency matrix mean? It is the number of walks of exactly k edges from vertex i to vertex j. Walks may revisit vertices and reuse edges, so the entry does not count paths. (2) How can the matrix show that a graph is connected? A graph with N ≥ 2 vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A² + … + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, (I + A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. The proof turns a positive entry into a walk, cuts the walk down to a path, and uses the fact that a path has at most N−1 edges. (3) What is a path? Under the course convention, a path is a walk with no repeated vertex. The textbook's own "path" means a walk with no repeated edge, which the note calls a trail. Small worked examples (a 3-town connected graph and a disconnected one) and a computer check on every graph with up to 6 vertices support the result. I can state all of this, but the amount of provenance material (board photos, textbook page numbers, a critique of another paper) made the core answer harder to find.
