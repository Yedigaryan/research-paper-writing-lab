READER SUMMARY
The note answers three questions about a graph's adjacency matrix A. First, the (i,j) entry of A^k is the number of walks of exactly k steps from vertex i to vertex j, where a walk may revisit vertices and edges. It does not count paths. For example, the diagonal of A^2 equals the vertex degrees, although no path of length 2 goes from a vertex back to itself. The proof is by induction on k, grouping walks by their second-to-last vertex. Second, a graph with N vertices is connected exactly when every off-diagonal entry of A + A^2 + ... + A^(N−1) is positive. Equivalently, the matrix (I + A)^(N−1) has no zero entry. This works because any walk between two vertices can be shortened to a path with at most N − 1 steps. Third, in this course a path is a walk that never repeats a vertex. The textbook uses "path" for a walk that never repeats an edge, and this note calls that a trail. For deciding connectivity the two kinds of route give the same answer. The paper tests everything on a connected 3-vertex chain and on a disconnected graph (one edge plus an isolated vertex). It also reports exhaustive computer checks for up to 6 vertices. It notes that the board's drawing and matrix differ by one edge, follows the matrix, and points out that some published sentences wrongly call walk counts "path" counts.
